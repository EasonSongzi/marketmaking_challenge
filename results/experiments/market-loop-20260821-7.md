### g1-expiry-settled-markout

- Hypothesis: Even measured from the true fill price, a markout that drops every option leaving the active book scores zero on exactly the trades whose profit or loss was actually realised - a binary option held to expiry pays one or zero and that terminal number is the only unambiguous verdict on the counterparty who gave us the trade. on_step_advance is the only hook that can see the previous underlying state and the set of contracts that just dropped out of the book, so settling those fills at their realised payoff rather than discarding them adds the terminal truth that both the parent and a fill-price-only markout are structurally blind to.
- Implementation plan: Target method is quote; on_trade, on_step_advance, MarketMaker helpers and appended __init__ state are all in scope. Steps (1), (2), (4) and (5) are identical to candidate g1-fill-price-markout and must be implemented the same way: append the same five constant container initialisers to __init__ without disturbing the existing prefix; make on_trade record (counterparty_id, option.option_id, quantity, price) into self._open_fills after the mandatory add_option_quantity call and never call price_option there; delete the five getattr blocks and the _settle_markout call from quote and bind quote_snapshots, fill_signals, counterparty_markout and request_counts directly from the __init__ attributes, leaving every other statement and every literal in quote byte-for-byte unchanged; and leave the now-unused helper removed or repurposed as the settlement helper. The single difference is step (3), on_step_advance. Before overwriting the state, capture the two things only this hook can see: previous_value_by_underlying_id, a dict comprehension over self.underlying_state mapping underlying_id to value, and expired_options, a dict mapping option_id to the BinaryOption for every option in self.active_option_state whose option_id is not present in new_option_state. Then perform the two mandatory assignments self.underlying_state = new_underlying_state and self.active_option_state = new_option_state, keeping the existing next(...) lookups and self._live_* accumulation statements ahead of them exactly as they are. After the assignments, return immediately if self.estimated_market_parameters is None. Otherwise build theo_by_option_id over the new self.active_option_state as in the other candidate, and rebuild self._open_fills with three cases per record: if option_id is in theo_by_option_id, credit quantity * (current_theo - entry_mark) to self._counterparty_markout[counterparty_id] and keep the record re-marked at current_theo; if option_id is in expired_options, credit quantity * (expired_options[option_id].expiry_valuation(previous_value_by_underlying_id) - entry_mark) and drop the record; otherwise drop the record without crediting. Use previous_value_by_underlying_id, not the new state, because a contract leaves the book after settling against the values that were current while it still carried zero steps to expiry, and price_option_from_parameters already prices a zero-step option as exactly expiry_valuation under the then-current state, so the terminal credit is consistent to the cent with what the parent would have marked had the contract been requoted on its last day. expiry_valuation reads leg.underlying_id out of the supplied dict, and the previous state always contains all three underlyings, so no KeyError is reachable. Do not iterate history in on_trade and do not add any per-fill structure that is not pruned here. Change no numeric literal in quote or respond_to_fok. Expected tradeoff: counterparties whose flow we held to expiry are finally scored, and the score is realised rather than modelled, which should be decisive on near-expiry sessions such as case 18 and on the FOK-dominated flow in case 7; the risk is that a single one-or-zero payoff is a much higher-variance verdict than a theo drift, so a counterparty can be mislabelled by one lucky settlement and the two-cent narrowing plus extra lot then applies to the wrong flow for the rest of the session.
- Worker summary: Identical to g1-fill-price-markout in __init__, on_trade, quote and the helper deletion; the only difference is on_step_advance. Before the mandatory reassignment it captures previous_value_by_underlying_id from the outgoing underlying state and expired_options for every contract in the outgoing book absent from new_option_state - the two things no other hook can see. After the reassignment and the None guard it settles three ways: surviving fills are credited theo drift and re-marked, fills on just-expired contracts are credited quantity * (expiry_valuation(previous_values) - entry_mark) and dropped, and anything else is dropped uncredited. Every literal and branch in quote is byte-for-byte unchanged. Compiled and passed scope validation against the r00 parent.

### g1-captured-edge

- Hypothesis: Forward markout is the wrong question for a per-counterparty statistic that has to converge inside a short session. What we can know exactly, at the instant of every fill and with no waiting and no forward marking, is the edge we captured: signed quantity multiplied by the difference between our own theo and the price actually transacted. A counterparty who repeatedly lifts us at prices we like is worth quoting tighter and larger regardless of where theo drifts afterwards, and by the convergence rule a statistic that gains one exact observation per fill is the kind that can support live adaptation, whereas a markout needs a day boundary and a surviving contract before it produces its first number at all.
- Implementation plan: Target method is quote; on_trade, on_step_advance, MarketMaker helpers and appended __init__ state are all in scope. (1) In __init__, append only constant initialisers after the existing body, which must survive as an exact prefix: self._counterparty_markout: dict = {}, self._quote_snapshots: dict = {}, self._fill_signals: dict = {}, self._quote_request_counts: dict = {}. Do not add an open-fill list; this candidate keeps no per-fill history at all. Do not remove or reorder any existing __init__ statement. (2) In on_trade, keep self.position.add_option_quantity(option.option_id, quantity) as the first statement. Then, guarded by self.estimated_market_parameters is not None so that no fill before warm_up can raise, compute theoretical_value = self.price_option(option) and accumulate self._counterparty_markout[counterparty_id] = self._counterparty_markout.get(counterparty_id, 0.0) + quantity * (theoretical_value - price). The sign is correct for both directions without a branch: a buy carries positive quantity and pays price, so the term is positive when we bought below theo, and a sell carries negative quantity and receives price, so the term is positive when we sold above theo. This is exactly one price_option call per fill, which is what on_trade already costs in the parent, and it keeps the hook O(1) with no growing structure. Delete the getattr lazy-init of _open_trades and its append. (3) In on_step_advance, change nothing at all: keep the four next(...) lookups, the three self._live_* accumulation statements and both mandatory assignments exactly as they are. This candidate deliberately has no day-boundary component, which is what makes it the structural opposite of the other two arms. (4) In quote, delete the five getattr lazy-initialiser blocks for _quote_snapshots, _fill_signals, _open_trades, _counterparty_markout and _quote_request_counts along with the self._settle_markout(open_trades, counterparty_markout) call, and bind the local names directly from the attributes declared in __init__: quote_snapshots = self._quote_snapshots, fill_signals = self._fill_signals, counterparty_markout = self._counterparty_markout, request_counts = self._quote_request_counts. The local name open_trades is no longer needed in quote; remove it. Keep the request_counts binding at its current position so repeat_request is computed from the same state as today. Every other statement in quote - the prior-quote position-delta reconstruction that writes fill_signals, the half_width chain and all its literals, label_depth_applies, both arms of the narrowing branch including the counterparty_markout.get(counterparty_id, 0.0) > 0.0 test, the wing block, the fill-signal cent, all capacity arithmetic and every quantity branch - must be byte-for-byte unchanged, and the dictionary keeps its name so the consumer is textually identical to the parent's. (5) Delete the _settle_markout helper; nothing calls it in this candidate. Change no numeric literal anywhere in quote or respond_to_fok. Expected tradeoff: the statistic is exact, available from the very first fill of the session including every FOK fill, and cannot be polluted by drift that has nothing to do with the counterparty; the risk is that it measures our own pricing rather than the counterparty's information, so a counterparty who systematically picks us off at prices just inside our theo will register as good and receive tighter, larger quotes, which would show as a PnL loss concentrated in the sessions with the most adverse flow.
- Worker summary: The structural opposite arm: no per-fill history and no day-boundary component at all. on_step_advance is byte-for-byte identical to the parent. on_trade, guarded by estimated_market_parameters is not None, accumulates quantity * (self.price_option(option) - price) straight into _counterparty_markout in O(1), which is positive for a buy below theo and, because quantity is signed, positive for a sell above theo without a branch. __init__ declares four containers, quote binds them directly, and the _settle_markout call and helper are gone. The consumer dictionary keeps its name so both narrowing and quantity-bump sites are textually identical to the parent. Compiled and passed scope validation against the r00 parent.
