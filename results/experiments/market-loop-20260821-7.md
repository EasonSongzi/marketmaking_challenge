### g1-expiry-settled-markout

- Hypothesis: Even measured from the true fill price, a markout that drops every option leaving the active book scores zero on exactly the trades whose profit or loss was actually realised - a binary option held to expiry pays one or zero and that terminal number is the only unambiguous verdict on the counterparty who gave us the trade. on_step_advance is the only hook that can see the previous underlying state and the set of contracts that just dropped out of the book, so settling those fills at their realised payoff rather than discarding them adds the terminal truth that both the parent and a fill-price-only markout are structurally blind to.

The single difference is step (3), on_step_advance. Before overwriting the state, capture the two things only this hook can see: previous_value_by_underlying_id, a dict comprehension over self.underlying_state mapping underlying_id to value, and expired_options, a dict mapping option_id to the BinaryOption for every option in self.active_option_state whose option_id is not present in new_option_state. Then perform the two mandatory assignments self.underlying_state = new_underlying_state and self.active_option_state = new_option_state, keeping the existing next(...) lookups and self._live_* accumulation statements ahead of them exactly as they are. After the assignments, return immediately if self.estimated_market_parameters is None. Otherwise build theo_by_option_id over the new self.active_option_state as in the other candidate, and rebuild self._open_fills with three cases per record: if option_id is in theo_by_option_id, credit quantity * (current_theo - entry_mark) to self._counterparty_markout[counterparty_id] and keep the record re-marked at current_theo; if option_id is in expired_options, credit quantity * (expired_options[option_id].expiry_valuation(previous_value_by_underlying_id) - entry_mark) and drop the record; otherwise drop the record without crediting. - Worker summary: Identical to g1-fill-price-markout in __init__, on_trade, quote and the helper deletion; the only difference is on_step_advance. Before the mandatory reassignment it captures previous_value_by_underlying_id from the outgoing underlying state and expired_options for every contract in the outgoing book absent from new_option_state - the two things no other hook can see. After the reassignment and the None guard it settles three ways: surviving fills are credited theo drift and re-marked, fills on just-expired contracts are credited quantity * (expiry_valuation(previous_values) - entry_mark) and dropped, and anything else is dropped uncredited.
