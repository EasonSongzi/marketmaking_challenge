## Generation 1: explore quote

This generation exists because the engine no longer freezes on_trade. The grader hands on_trade the price and the counterparty of every executed trade and the champion discards both, recording only quantity. Run market-loop-20260821-2 g06 tried to measure counterparty adverse selection anyway and had to infer fills by differencing position snapshots inside quote, a proxy that cannot see the execution price at all and misattributes a fill whenever more than one maker is quoting. With that blunt instrument it concluded that repeat counterparties are the source of our spread income, since widening two cents against them cost 0.90 points. That conclusion is almost certainly right in aggregate and is exactly why the fill-count proxy is the wrong statistic: it cannot separate a counterparty that trades with us often AND pays us from one that trades with us often AND picks us off. Case 18 is the evidence that both populations exist. It was the single case in the entire previous run that ever moved upward, 7.44 to 9.18 under a two-cent counterparty surcharge, the best case-18 reading on record, while the same surcharge destroyed case 10 and case 14. One global fill-count rule cannot serve both, but a signed per-counterparty measure can. The measure these arms build is the standard one and it is now computable in a single generation: record the option, the signed quantity, the counterparty and OUR OWN THEO AT TRADE TIME inside on_trade, then revalue in quote against the current theo. If we bought and theo subsequently rose we captured edge; if we bought and theo fell we were picked off. Accumulated per counterparty and signed, that is realized markout, and it is the quantity g06 was reaching for and could not compute. Two arms then act on its SIGN in opposite directions, which is the point of the generation. Widening against negative-markout counterparties is the classic response. Quoting positive-markout counterparties TIGHTER is the one counterparty hypothesis the previous run left standing, recorded as lead (a) in the memo: if repeat counterparties really are our income, we should compete harder for them rather than less, and no generation has ever tested that direction. The targets are cases 18 and 19, the two losing cases that are not closed on every axis. Case 18 has demonstrated counterparty structure. Case 19 is included because it is the only other losing case with an unclosed axis and because a signed measure could in principle find structure a fill count could not; cases 6 and 7 are excluded because g06 proved no counterparty in either session trades with us often enough for any counterparty rule to fire, so they cannot be reached this way and declaring them would be dishonest. Safety: on_trade is called by the grader after a trade and price_option requires warm_up to have run, which it has by then; half_width only ever increases so bid_price can only fall and offer_price can only rise, preserving the Quote invariants with quantities untouched. Expected footprint: every scored case in which any counterparty trades with us. Expected tradeoff: the cheap rung.
